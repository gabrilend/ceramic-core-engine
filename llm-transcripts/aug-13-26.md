meaning *nobody has said yet*. The readiness dispatch answers no for it forever,
so a station holding one waits rather than running — that's what lets a
station exist before it's finished being wired. Cells are allocated when a
station is placed, sized from the registry, and are never freed until the map
is, so changing what a port is for is a field write. A port can be told how deep
to start; ten otherwise.


The part worth calling out: binding a static used to **free the cell array**,
which silently discarded whatever a producer had already handed over and nobody
had claimed. Somebody turning a port from a wire into a constant lost the values
waiting in it and found out never. That's fixed.


**A state on every cell (210c), steps 1–2.** Each cell carries its own state
— nothing here / a writer is filling me / the bytes have landed / a reader is
emptying me — and every move between them is one atomic swap naming the state
it starts from. Two threads can never own a cell, and a move out of a state a
cell isn't in is impossible rather than discouraged. The copies stay inside the
lock deliberately, so a bug in the machine surfaces as a refused move that stops
the program rather than as a torn value that doesn't.


**The scan (210d), steps 1–3.** Head and tail are gone. A reader looks for a
usable cell instead of computing where one must be. That's the sentence
everything downstream rests on: *a position must be exact and is therefore
computed; a hint may be wrong and therefore is not.* A hint needs no exclusion
to stay true, because nothing about it must be.
